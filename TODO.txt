Best sequence now:

Collect and inspect frames first.
Use run_eval.py with dumping, then review overlays in inspect_vision.py. This confirms your grenade growth cue is visible and consistently captured.

Label a small pilot set before full dataset.
Label about 200 to 400 frames with your new 3 classes from detector.py: ENEMY, GRENADE, PROJECTILE. If labels are noisy, training quality will be noisy no matter what model you use.

Run a short YOLO pilot training.
Do a quick low-cost run to validate signal quality, confusion patterns, and whether GRENADE is separable from PROJECTILE in your captures.

Only then scale up to full training.
If pilot metrics and visual predictions look good, increase data and epochs.

Why this order:

It derisks everything cheaply.
It verifies your key hypothesis now: grenade size growth across captures is a learnable temporal/appearance cue.
It avoids spending time on a full train with bad labels or bad frame mix.
Practical checkpoint:

If pilot inference on held-out dumps clearly finds grenades and reduces your current false positives, proceed to full YOLO.
If not, improve labeling consistency and sample diversity first (especially near ambiguous projectile frames).
